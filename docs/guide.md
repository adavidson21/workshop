# Using Workshop

What each app does and how a day flows through them. To install, see the [README](../README.md); for every install option, [install.md](install.md).

## The apps

- **Needs you** (a button in the top bar on every app; app id `home`, formerly the Home page) opens a panel over the app you're in: what needs you in any app, what's coming up, what you finished this week (Weekly status) and Get started. Its count is on the button. It shows and links; work happens in each app, and Docket's Today is where the day happens. Workshop opens on the first app on the rail (Docket). Guide: [apps/home/README.md](../apps/home/README.md).
- **Docket** (formerly Prioritizer; code and URLs still say `prioritizer`) decides what to work on next (work items assigned to you and pull requests waiting on your review become tasks when the ADO connector is on), User guide: [Docket's README](../apps/prioritizer/README.md).
- **Dispatch** (formerly Agents; code and URLs still say `agents`) is the front end for [Tandem](https://github.com/adavidson21/eng-agents) (the eng-agents repo), the agent scaffolding that drives opencode. It shows your work items and the opencode agents working on them, and runs the next step. It needs Workshop running on this computer with the opencode connector on.
- **Rapport** (formerly Receipts, with 1:1s inside since 2.0.0) keeps receipts and 1:1s for you, your reports and your manager: your own receipts for a self review (the main use), each direct report's page with your 1:1 prep and note above their receipts, a 1:1s tab with everyone you meet with by who is due, a Goals tab with the team's and each report's OKRs as a tree (one plain `.md` file per owner per quarter, key results updated in place), optional upward notes, and review drafts grouped by your review form's sections. 1:1 prep gathers what Docket and Rapport know since your last 1:1; notes are plain `.md` files in your Workshop folder; after the meeting, follow-ups are copied as Docket quick-add lines and wins become receipts. Drafting talking points with your opencode agents is optional. Its code lives in its own repo, [adavidson21/receipts](../apps/receipts/README.md), with its own site, https://receipts.alexisdavidson.org; Workshop serves it under `/apps/receipts/` like Docket and Runway (apps/remote.json). Guide: that repo's README.
- **Runway** is team and project roadmaps: type an outline or drag a timeline, export an image or Markdown. Ad hoc roadmaps need nothing else; with the ADO connector on it can also import Epics and Features to edit, or show a live ADO view. Its code lives in [adavidson21/runway](../apps/runway/README.md) and is served here under `/apps/runway/`.
- **Slate** is your team's backlog in Azure DevOps and your own ideas, on one board of six columns: **Inbox**, **Shape** (triage, waiting on the requester, an interview with you, the brief), **Define**, **Who builds it?**, **In flight** (delegated, or building in Tandem through review and PR to merged) and **Resolved**. Tandem's `/triage`, `/shape`, `/refine`, `/scope`, `/slice` and `/handoff` do each step's legwork from a button; each step shows at most three buttons, with the rest in **More**. **Health** shows where requests stall, who holds delegated work, **Sprints** (move and assign ADO items by sprint), and Runway's team reports. Guide: [apps/slate/README.md](../apps/slate/README.md).
- **Folio** (formerly Notes, then Notebook; app id `notes`) is your notebook of Markdown files, laid out like OneNote: section tabs along the top (Notes, Daily, Products, Templates, and Tandem), the section's pages on the left, and the page. Links with backlinks, tags, search, images you paste in, templates you can change in place, a daily note, version history, links to other apps' records (`ws:` links), and action items and wins you can send to Docket and Rapport. The Tandem section shows the plans, specs, reviews and briefs Tandem writes in `.work`, read and edited in place through the opencode connector (`docs.list`, `file.read`, `file.write`), so nothing is copied; Dispatch's file sheet edits the same files and has **Open in Folio**. Files are plain `.md` with standard links, so they work in any Markdown tool. Its code lives in its own repo, [adavidson21/notes](../apps/notes/README.md), with its own site, https://notes.alexisdavidson.org; Workshop serves it under `/apps/notes/` like Docket, Runway and Rapport (apps/remote.json). Guide: that repo's README.
- **Settings** has a left nav grouped by task. **Workshop**: Overview (connector status with a link to whatever needs you, the health check), Apps, This computer (start at login, work mode, pairing, port), Data & backups. **People & teams**: everyone you assign work to (you, your manager, reports, peers, contacts) and groups of them (`@leadership`), shared by every app, added one at a time or from a spreadsheet you fill in and import after a preview; and your team's Azure DevOps area paths. **Azure DevOps**: Connections, Work items (types, states, team report counting), Pull requests (where Docket looks, your teams, AI first-pass reviews). **Code & agents**: Products (the products you work on, with other names, repos and area paths; Folio makes each one's product note, filled in from here, for you to edit, [products.md](products.md)), Repos (add a repo, choose its ADO project) and Agents (opencode and Tandem). **Help**: User guide, a list of "I need to..." situations (break a goal into stories, get a simple change built, fix a bug, review PRs and more), each with the best way to do it, why, and another way when there is one (`apps/settings/js/guide.js`). Each page saves only its own fields. Azure DevOps and opencode are optional: without them their pages say what to do in one line, and the rest works.

## Through the day

Each part of the day has one obvious place to start:

- **Morning: Docket's Today.** Workshop opens there. **Needs you** in the top bar lists everything waiting on you across apps in one place: agents asking a question, Slate items to decide or approve, pull requests to review, 1:1s due, PRs blocked on you, follow-ups due.
- **Any time: Capture.** The **Capture** button in the top bar, or `Ctrl+.` (`Cmd+.` on a Mac), from any app: `t` a task, `w` waiting on someone, `r` a receipt, `n` a note, `p` a roadmap idea, `?` ask Tandem, `>` go to a place. It works the same everywhere.
- **New requests: Slate.** Triage, define, publish, then delegate or build it yourself.
- **Building: Dispatch.** Your Tandem runs, their questions, code review and push, and first-pass reviews of teammates' PRs.
- **1:1s: Rapport.** Prep shows what Docket, Slate and Rapport know since last time.
- **End of day: Wrap up** on Docket's Today. **Fridays: Weekly status** in Needs you drafts your update from what you finished in Docket, decided in Slate, shipped with Dispatch and won in Rapport.

Switch apps from the rail, or with `Alt+1`, `Alt+2` and so on. `Ctrl+K` (`Cmd+K` on a Mac) opens the command palette: every app's commands, from anywhere. Each app keeps its state while you're in another one. The first app is the plain address; the others are `#/<app>`, and `#/<app>/<path>` opens an app at a place, for example `#/agents/<work item id>` or `#/prioritizer/task/<task id>`. App names work too (`#/docket/today`, `#/rapport`). Apps that report where they are (Docket) keep the address up to date, so Back, Forward, refresh and bookmarks land on the same view or task.

**Pop out:** the pop-out button in the top bar (or *Pop out ...* in `Ctrl+K`) opens the app you're in as its own window, without the rail, for example Folio beside Docket. It's the same Workshop, so the same data. Folio opens on the note you had open. Links to other apps open in the main Workshop window, and the button in the pop-out's top bar (**Back to Workshop**) puts the app back and closes the window. The address is `?solo=<app id>#/<app id>`, so you can bookmark it.
